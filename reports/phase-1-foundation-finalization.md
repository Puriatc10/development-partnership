## 3. Key Changes & Approved Decisions Reflected

### 3.1. Pre-Operational Status
- Clearly recorded in every document that the business is in a **pre-operational foundation-building stage**.
- Explicitly documented that the planned technical team is **not yet onboarded and not currently available for client delivery**.
- Strictly established that agents and systems must never represent the business as actively delivering client work.

### 3.2. Planned Technical Team Structure
- Documented the approved structure of **four technical contributors**:
  - One more experienced technical contributor (Technical Lead / Architecture & QA).
  - Three junior developers (AI-assisted implementation specialists).
- Explicitly separated established structural facts from parameters awaiting determination (`TBD`).

### 3.3. Initial Service Focus & Feasibility Review
- Codified three strategic initial service priorities:
  1. *Backend and API Development* (Endpoints, auth, database-backed apps, integrations).
  2. *Webflow Extensions and Integrations* (Backend connectivity, external DBs, auth, webhooks).
  3. *Framer Custom Development* (React custom components, application logic, APIs, interactive features).
- Explicitly established that these represent **strategic commercial priorities**, not verified team capabilities.
- Mandated **technical feasibility review** by technical leadership before commercial acceptance of any project scope.
- Formally deferred standard commercial positioning for:
  - Native mobile application development (iOS/Android).
  - Complex enterprise systems (large legacy/distributed systems).
  - Complex custom e-commerce platforms (multi-vendor marketplaces/logistics engines).

### 3.4. Commercial Model Hierarchy
- Established the strategic sequence:
  1. **Project-Based Wholesale Development:** Initial entry model to establish trust with low partner friction.
  2. **Monthly Reserved Development Capacity:** Primary recurring revenue objective, offered once trust and consistent demand are established.
  3. **Strategic Revenue Sharing:** Selective arrangement for vetted partners; strictly prohibited in cold outreach.

### 3.5. Revenue Sharing Governance
- Removed speculative equity-sharing language.
- Codified that revenue-sharing agreements must explicitly define the calculation basis: recognized revenue, deductions, direct development costs, collection timing, refunds, scope boundaries, reporting, and audit rights.
- Established that engineering costs and delivery risks must be legally and commercially protected.
- Percentage ranges remain marked `TBD`.

### 3.6. Partner & End-Client Protection
- Codified that the **partner owns the commercial relationship** with its end client.
- Enforced strict **non-circumvention** and **zero direct commercial solicitation** of partner clients.
- Prohibited direct communication with a partner’s end client without explicit prior written authorization.
- Established **behind-the-scenes white-label** as the default delivery model, with co-branded delivery permitted only by explicit mutual written agreement.

### 3.7. Proposed Default Project Delivery Standards
- Established internal proposed default operational standards:
  - Mandatory written Scope of Work (SOW) before project kickoff.
  - Explicit deliverables and milestone acceptance criteria.
  - Mandatory documented Change Request and commercial approval for out-of-scope work.
  - Proposed default of **two revision rounds** for in-scope work.
  - Proposed milestone acceptance inspection window of **five business days**.
  - Requirement of an agreed upfront payment before project kickoff.
- Explicitly noted that these are internal proposed defaults, not executed legal contracts.

### 3.8. Target Market Segmentation
- Separated initial target markets into three distinct profiles with tailored pain points and technical criteria:
  1. *Digital Agencies*
  2. *Webflow Studios*
  3. *Framer Studios*
- Added partner qualification criteria: project scoping maturity, technical literacy, recurring demand signals, and contractual maturity.
